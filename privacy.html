Briefly privacy policy

Last updated 22nd November 2026

Briefly is a Mac app that rewrites documents at a shorter length or a simpler reading level. It is made by Andrew Parnell.

The short version

Briefly does not collect, store or share any information about you, and your documents never leave your Mac.

Your documents

Briefly reads the documents you open (PDF, Word, text or Markdown files) and writes its versions using Apple Intelligence, the language model built into macOS, running on your Mac. Briefly itself has no access to the internet: the app is not allowed to make network connections, so it cannot send your documents, or anything else, anywhere.

The versions Briefly writes are saved on your Mac, inside the app's own storage, so they open instantly next time. You can delete them at any time in Briefly › Settings › General › Clear Saved Versions, or by deleting the app. If you choose to save a Briefly file or export a version, it goes where you choose, like any other document.

Data collection

Briefly collects no data. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking.

If Briefly crashes and you have chosen to share crash reports with developers in macOS settings, Apple may pass an anonymous crash report to the developer. You can change this in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.

Apple Intelligence

Apple Intelligence is part of macOS and is covered by Apple's own privacy policy. Briefly uses the on-device model only.

Children

Briefly collects no information from anyone, including children.

Changes
